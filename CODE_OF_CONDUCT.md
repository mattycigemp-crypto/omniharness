# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people and assume they are being straight with you. Argue about
the work, not the person doing it.

## What that means here

Most of what happens in this repository is disagreement about technical
decisions, which is the point. Disagreement is welcome. Contempt is not.

Some specifics, because "be nice" is not actionable:

**Say what you mean.** If a change is wrong, say it is wrong and why. Vague
disapproval is harder to answer than a blunt objection, and it wastes more of
everyone's time than plain speech would.

**Criticise the work.** "This does not handle the symlink case" is a review.
"Did you even test this" is not — and it is a worse review, because it does not
say what is broken.

**Being right does not license being unpleasant.** A correct patch delivered
with contempt costs more than it is worth. This applies most to people who are
usually right.

**Take being wrong well.** Everyone here ships bugs. Several were shipped by
people who then wrote the test that caught them. Someone finding your mistake is
doing you a favour, whatever tone they used.

**Assume competence.** People come to this project from different languages,
platforms and levels of experience. Not knowing something is not a character
flaw, and explaining it is not a burden.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, or abuse — public or private. Demeaning comments
about anyone's identity, background or beliefs. Sexual attention or imagery.
Publishing someone's private information without their consent. Sustained
disruption of discussion.

Any of these will get your comment deleted and, repeated, will get you blocked.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commits, code review, and anywhere else
you are representing the project. It does not extend to what you do elsewhere on
the internet, with the obvious exception of conduct directed at people here.

## Reporting

Report through [a private security advisory](https://github.com/shipking-ai/omniharness/security/advisories/new).
It is the private channel this repository has, and it reaches the maintainers
without becoming public. Say what happened and link to it if it is on GitHub.

You will get a reply. Reports stay between you and the maintainers.

If the person you need to report is a maintainer, GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) exists for exactly
that, and does not go through us.

## Enforcement

The maintainers decide, and will say what they decided and roughly why.
Depending on what happened, that ranges from a private word, to deleting a
comment, to a temporary or permanent block.

This is a small project. There is no committee and no appeals process — which
is a reason to be careful about how these decisions are made, not a licence to
make them carelessly.
